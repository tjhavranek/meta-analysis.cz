12 November 2026Zuzana HavránkováLinkedIn

What is the optimal inflation rate? We have pre-printed a meta-analysis summarizing 777 estimates from 116 studies that try to provide an answer.

Our best guess is that this literature is consistent with optimal inflation of about 0.6%. This might have come as a disappointment to some of my co-authors, but for different reasons. One of them has spent a lot of time arguing for zero inflation on average. Another spent many years as a central banker defending the standard 2% target. Well, you can't make everyone happy!

The estimates differ a lot, and we show which modelling choices drive the differences.

This is our first meta-analysis where we really used AI a lot for data collection, although of course the AI workflow there already feels like pre-history. The prompts are public if you want to see how it was done.

Disclaimer: most of these optimal inflation estimates are not estimates in the classical sense but come from calibrated models, so publication bias corrections and other meta-analysis approaches are a bit tricky here.

Measurement error matters too: official inflation can overstate true inflation (it doesn't fully capture better quality and new products) or understate it (the cost of owning a home is often only partly captured or left out). Depending on how big the net bias is and how you read the models, the gap between 0.6% and 2% can shrink, grow, or even flip. We discuss this in the paper but perhaps that's a good topic for another meta-analysis.

Joint work with amazing co-authors Matej Opatrny, Martin Opatrný, Tomas Havranek, and Mojmír Hampl.

Tomas Havranek is Professor of Economics at the Institute of Economic Studies, Charles University, Prague. Zuzana Irsova is Professor of Economics at the same institute. She works on meta-analysis and meta-research, labour economics and international economics, and is an affiliate researcher at Stanford METRICS. She writes in Czech as Zuzana Havránková.
