2. WHAT WE DO NOT LICENSE
-------------------------

2a. THE ORIGINAL RESEARCH DATASETS
    Each dataset under a project folder was assembled for a specific published
    paper, by an author team that in most cases extends beyond the maintainers of
    this site. **Their rights status is unchanged by this file.** Nothing here
    purports to relicense a co-author's contribution, and no reuse right in the
    underlying data is granted by section 1b.

    `/api/v1/datasets.json` records a `rights_status` and, where one is known, a
    `license_url` for every dataset. Where the status is `unspecified`, no open
    licence has been established and you should treat the data as all rights
    reserved unless the paper or its publisher says otherwise.

2b. THE CSV AND PARQUET MIRRORS
    The files under `/data/v1/<project>/` are **format conversions** of the
    corresponding source dataset. A format conversion does not create a new
    licence. Their rights status is inherited from the source dataset in 2a, not
    granted by section 1b.

2c. HISTORICAL REPLICATION CODE
    The `.do`, `.R` and other scripts distributed with the papers were written by
    those papers' author teams. **They are not covered by the MIT grant in 1a.**
    An earlier version of this file appeared to license them; that was an
    overreach and is corrected here.

2d. THIRD-PARTY MATERIAL INSIDE REPLICATION PACKAGES
    Some packages embed material from other sources with its own terms. Two are
    distributed here that are not extracted-estimate datasets of ours: the crisis
    database under `ews/` and the cross-field archive bundled in the MAIVE
    replication package under `maive/`. Their original terms apply.

2e. THE PAPERS
    PDFs are author copies for scholarly use. Copyright in the published versions
    rests with the respective journals.


3. HOW TO CITE
--------------

Cite BOTH, not one or the other:

  1. the collection —
     Havránek, T. and Z. Iršová (2026). meta-analysis.cz: data and code for
     meta-analyses in economics. https://meta-analysis.cz

  2. the paper whose data you used — `datasets.json` carries its title, authors
     and DOI for exactly this purpose.

Attribution is not the same as permission. Citing a paper tells readers whose
work it is; it does not by itself establish that you hold a reuse right in that
paper's data. Where that matters for your use, check `rights_status` and, if it
is `unspecified`, ask the authors.


4. IF YOU ARE A CO-AUTHOR
-------------------------

If you co-authored one of these datasets and want its rights status changed —
in either direction — say so and it will be recorded in `datasets.json` and
honoured here. The default is deliberately conservative.
